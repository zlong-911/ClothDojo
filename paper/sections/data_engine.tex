\section{NOCS-Guided Data Engine}
\label{sec:data-engine}

\subsection{Privileged trajectory agent}

NOCS exposes canonical garment geometry through an image-like observation. It
makes successful behavior easier to learn while preserving the VLA observation
and action structure. Corresponding regions retain related visual coordinates
across different garments, giving the agent cues about local identity, surface
orientation, and canonical arrangement without introducing a separate symbolic
action representation. Human demonstrations initialize the trajectory agent;
we denote the resulting SFT parameters by $\theta_{\mathrm{init}}$. Rollout and
HIL data then improve it.

At time $t$, the policy $\pi_\theta$ receives NOCS images, robot state, and a
task instruction, and predicts an action chunk $A_t$. The simulator executes
the chunk from state $s_t$ and returns the next observation. Task metrics
measure shape and orientation for flattening or fold quality for folding,
classifying rollouts as successful ($\tau^+$) or failed ($\tau^-$).

\subsection{Fixed-target trajectory generation}

A generation target is identified by a garment and a fixed random initial
state. A failed target is retained for diagnosis and HIL rather than replaced
by an easier random state. This keeps large-scale successful data tied to the
original initial-state distribution. The agent's success rate and the final
coverage of difficult states consequently remain observable rather than being
hidden by repeated resampling.

For a selected rollout, a reviewer marks an intervention state, the simulator
restores that recorded state, and an operator resumes VR teleoperation. The
resulting correction $\mathcal{D}_H$ retains its source rollout and intervention
point. Corrections are aggregated with the original expert demonstrations for
supervised fine-tuning with the same action-prediction objective used for
initialization. Autonomous rollouts provide states for correction; only
demonstrated actions enter this policy update. The updated policy then
generates further rollouts.

\subsection{Selection and visual replay}

Successful rollouts may contain failure and recovery. They are trimmed at the
best valid success state, then replayed under different visual conditions. This
separates new state--action behavior from new visual observations of the same
behavior. New rollouts expand the physical state--action distribution;
re-rendering expands only the visual distribution. Keeping these identities
separate makes their downstream effects experimentally distinguishable.

Re-rendering restores the recorded states, keeping robot and garment motion
and action targets unchanged. It varies garment materials, scene appearance,
and lighting between episodes while keeping each episode visually consistent.

\subsection{Released data contract}

The downstream dataset primarily contains autonomous successful trajectories.
Failures and interventions are used to improve the trajectory agent. Every
sample retains its garment, initial state, physical trajectory, rendering, and
provenance identities so later experimental splits remain auditable.
